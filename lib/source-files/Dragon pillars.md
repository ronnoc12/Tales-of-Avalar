Dragon pillars 

\#\# 🜂 \*\*The Ten Pillars of the Ember Hall\*\*

Each pillar is 10 feet tall and faced with cracked tiles of gemstone and volcanic glass, faintly reflective in the magma’s glow. The earliest pillars are bleached and worn smooth; the newer ones retain sharp edges and richer pigment. All are carved with runes in ancient Draconic that flicker faintly when touched.

\---

\#\#\# \*\*Pillar  1– The Breath of the First Dawn\*\*

\*Style: Oldest, near-erased. Silver and white marble tiles faded to ivory.\*

A radiant serpentine figure spirals from a disc of light in the sky — \*\*Bahamut\*\* in symbolic form — exhaling motes of flame, frost, storm, and shadow that fall like seeds into the world below. Around the base, four smaller wyrms coil in slumber, their scales of red, blue, green, and gold only barely visible beneath dust and age.

\> \*Implied Meaning:\* The divine creation — the birth of dragons as embodiments of the world’s elements.

\---

\#\#\# \*\*Pillar 2 – The Shaping of the Lands\*\*

\*Style: Early, fractured but vibrant blues and greens.\*

Dragons of every hue soar across oceans and mountains, their breath shaping continents — a red dragon’s fire melts new valleys; a white one freezes rivers; a bronze one calls lightning that splits the sky. Below them, small figures — mortal races — build shrines and raise hands in awe.

\> \*Implied Meaning:\* Dragons once ruled as gods of creation and change — revered and feared equally.

\---

\#\#\# \*\*Pillar 3 – The Rival Thrones\*\*

\*Style: Mid-aged, fiery red tones dominate.\*

Two dragons, both immense, lock claws midair above a burning plain — one silver, one crimson. Around them, lesser dragons clash in storms of elemental fury, while mortals flee below. Between them hangs a shattered crown of stone.

\> \*Implied Meaning:\* The Age of Dragon Wars — brotherhood turning to dominion, the gods’ children fighting for supremacy.\*

\---

\#\#\# \*\*Pillar 4 – The Celestial Judgment\*\*

\*Style: Gilded edges, faint celestial motifs.\*

A radiant humanoid silhouette, possibly \*\*Bahamut\*\*, looms in the heavens above kneeling dragons. Light cascades downward, fracturing into seven beams — each touching a dragon of a different color. One turns away in pride while others bow in submission.

\> \*Implied Meaning:\* The first divine rift — pride separating dragonkind from their maker.\*

\---  
\#\#\# \*\*Pillar 5 – The Age of Flame and Shadow\*\*

\*Dark, oppressive. Blacks and reds dominate.\*

Humanoids toil beneath towering dragons. They drag chains of gold and heaps of gems toward colossal draconic thrones. One mosaic panel shows a mother shielding her child from a dragon’s gaze as fire scorches the horizon. Above, Bahamut’s sigil — cracked and dim — watches in silence, a single silver tear falling. A cruel looking red dragon now appears enthroned, its claws dripping with molten coin as it grins menacingly.

\> \*Implied Meaning:\* The age of fear — mortals enslaved, their lives reduced to tribute; the gods dismayed by their children’s tyranny.\*

\---

\#\#\# \*\*Pillar 6 – The Betrayer’s Blade\*\*

\*Style: Sharper detail, newer craft, darker tones.\*

A \*\*shadowy humanoid figure\*\* stands atop a mountain of dragon corpses, its features indistinct. The blade it wields drips with red and black glass shards. The dragons above writhe in agony — their wings torn, their hearts pierced by light that looks like binding chains.

\> \*Implied Meaning:\* The unknown destroyer — perhaps Vhalion’s order, or the first dragon slayer. It’s intentionally ambiguous.\*

—

\#\#\# \*\*Pillar 7 – The Fall of Fire\*\*

\*Grim, visceral, and fresh in color as if recently restored.\*

Mortals — countless armies — swarm across a battlefield of flame and ruin. Massive siege engines hurl chains wreathed in light; ballistae pierce wings; wizards rain spells like arrows. In the center lies a dying red dragon — vast, majestic even in death — its scales cracked and smoking. Mortals cheer upon its corpse while \*\*a clutch of hatchlings\*\* huddle beneath one broken wing, eyes glowing faintly with fear and hatred.

\> \*Implied Meaning:\* The annihilation of dragonkind; mortals’ triumph shadowed by tragedy.\*

\#\#\# \*\*Pillar 8 – The Shattered Pantheon\*\*

\*Style: Cracked, jagged art. Pieces missing or melted.\*

The image shows temples collapsing, draconic idols desecrated, and the symbol of Bahamut darkened. Magma seeps from the cracks as if the carving itself bleeds. A single silver eye watches from the void, tears of molten light falling into ruin.

\> \*Implied Meaning:\* The death of faith — Bahamut’s temples abandoned, dragons’ extinction nearly complete.\*

\---

\#\#\# \*\*Pillar 9 – The Egg and the Grove\*\*

\*Style: Serene mosaic, natural greens and silvers, unusually peaceful.\*

A cluster of \*\*five dragon eggs\*\*, each a different color, rest nestled within the roots of a massive tree in a tranquil grove. Tiny runes in the bark shimmer faintly — when translated, they’re the coordinates or features of an unfamiliar plane (a subtle hint to where the dragons went).

\> \*Implied Meaning:\* Hope reborn — the dragons’ legacy hidden, awaiting a time of unity.\*

—

 \#\#\# \*\*Pillar 10 – The Mocking Sovereign\*\*

\*Newer than the others, but chipped crudely as if made in spite.\*

A skeletal figure, stands triumphantly atop the same dragon’s remains. His robes flare in spectral fire as he holds aloft a goblet made from a dragon’s fang, pouring molten gold over the corpse in mock offering. In the background, lesser mages applaud, raising scrolls and banners. Carved along the bottom in fresh, cutting script: \*“Even gods bleed. Even dragons kneel.”\*

\> \*Implied Meaning:\* The victor’s mockery — Vhalion’s desecration of the dragon’s memory and Bahamut’s silence.\*

—
