**True name:** unknown to the party, who know her only as the Mother of Monsters. Fey guard their true names closely.

## Appearance
A semi-fey humanoid with unnervingly shifting features: opalescent skin, eyes that sometimes split like a dragon's, hair that drifts like smoke. Sometimes an old, bent crone; sometimes a beautiful, terrifyingly youthful fey; sometimes she subtly sprouts monstrous anatomy: scales, wings, or claws.

## Realm
The [[Feywild#Ossuary Wilds|Ossuary Wilds]]: a labyrinthine, mutable forest of organic horrors, glowing fungi, and rivers of black, viscous water, where trees shift position, roots whisper secrets, and grotesque, semi-intelligent creatures stalk the shadows.

## Personality
- **Ancient and unpredictable:** she has seen entire fey courts rise and fall, and her plans can span centuries.
- **Cunning and manipulative:** she nudges the other archfey toward conflict, chaos, or cooperation as it suits her, rarely acting directly.
- **Mentor and predator:** she "gifts" knowledge, artifacts, and strange bargains, always at a cost. Those who underestimate her pay with their freedom, memories, or lives.
- **Morally ambiguous:** she sees herself as the Feywild's caretaker, using cruelty to shape life the way a dragon uses fire to control a forest.

## Backstory
She emerged from the earliest chaos of the Feywild, long before the current archfey order, and has spent millennia experimenting with life, horror, and magic, blending fey, mortal, and draconic essence. She watched the dragons' exodus to the Feywild with fascination, and many of her later monstrosities borrow from draconic forms.

## The Black Dragon {secret}
She captured the black dragon [[Vhallion]] sent, and transformed it into her loyal agent and enforcer. Unknown to her, a fragment of its blood is locked in [[Nyxylia]]'s Crimson Vault, giving leverage she doesn't know exists.

## Court Influence {secret}
She uses her monsters as leverage in the Fey Court. The other archfey fear her but cannot openly confront her. She manipulates crises in neighboring domains to test their competence, plants doubt, suspicion, and envy so they act rashly or at cross-purposes, and keeps plausible deniability throughout. She sometimes poses as helpful or neutral, but the cost of her favors is always hidden until too late.

## With the Party
- She may trade a favor or artifact, such as an aether crystal, for something deeply personal: a memory, a dream, or a piece of their soul.
- She could mentor a party member, granting power or knowledge at a morally difficult cost.
- Her monsters may attack or aid the party unpredictably, depending on their actions and her experiments.

## Proposed Origin (not yet canon) {secret}

**Before the Sundering, the gentle hand.** She was once a compassionate, meticulous fey of adaptation, continuity, and balance through care: life is sacred because it is rare. She studied ecosystems like a healer: how creatures survive changing climates, how magic reshapes them, how extinction begins with neglect. She did not rule; she tended.

**Her faith and breaking point.** A devoted follower of [[Lathander]], she watched him create, fail, and move on: species burned away in divine trial-and-error, each time with *"It is regrettable. But dawn comes again."* To her it was waste. She came to believe the gods loved potential, not lives.

**The first changes.** She began helping creatures change before they needed to (tougher hides, altered instincts, faster reproduction), calling it mercy. But time eroded every advantage, and she learned that adaptation is temporary.

**The turning point** (cause open: a corrupting pre-Sundering sickness, a profound personal loss, or simple despair): *change must be permanent; survival must be enforced; waiting is death.* She stopped adapting creatures to environments and began adapting them against time itself. That is when they became monsters.

**Why she is cruel.** She doesn't hate life; she hates hesitation. Pain now beats extinction later; consent is meaningless when survival is at stake. Her rejection of the gods, especially Lathander, is quiet and absolute: she seeks to prove them wrong.

**Why not rule the Fey Court.** Rule is a distraction, the gods' short-term thinking wrapped in ceremony. She tolerates the Court for the stability she can work within.

**The tragedy.** Where Lathander discarded failures casually, she refuses to let anything fail at all, even when it should. She became exactly what she hated about the gods.
